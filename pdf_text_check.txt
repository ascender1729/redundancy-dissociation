Two Measurement Artifacts in a Sparse-Autoencoder Feature-Universality
Analysis, and What Survives

Anonymous ACL submission

Abstract

002
003
004

We report a reliability study of a natural-latent
prediction for sparse-autoencoder (SAE) features: that features which are redundant within
a model (recoverable from disjoint parts of
its own activations) are the ones that transfer across independently trained models. In
building the test we uncovered two measurement artifacts, each of which flips or fabricates the headline. (1) On large-activation
models an unfloored ridge R2 diverges to ∼
−1.4 × 105 for low-variance features, producing a spurious positive decoder-geometry
correlation (+0.27). (2) After that is fixed,
computing the partial correlation by residualizing on raw skewed controls (rather than
on ranks) fabricates a near-zero decoder result (+0.001) where the standard rank-based
partial Spearman gives −0.12. Under a single frozen, reviewer-reproducible estimator, the
surviving result is a dissociation: within-model
redundancy is weakly but consistently negatively associated with decoder-geometry universality across three model pairs (N =2500
features per pair; partial ρ ∈ [−0.17, −0.12],
all outside a permutation null), yet positively
associated with co-firing universality on two
of three pairs (ρ = +0.29, +0.17; one null) though only one of the three pairs clears the
pre-registered +0.20 confirmation threshold,
so the co-firing prediction is confirmed on one
of three pairs, not two. We release per-feature
arrays and a recompute script so every number
regenerates on a CPU in seconds. The practical lesson: within-model redundancy is not a
proxy for cross-model representational geometry, and interpretability correlation studies are
dangerously sensitive to the ridge floor and the
partialling convention.

Feature universality - independently trained networks learning overlapping features - is widely
reported (Olah et al., 2020; Lan et al., 2024a,b)

042
043

but lacks a predictor of which features transfer.
Natural-latent theory (Wentworth and Lorell, 2025)
suggests one: redundant latents are stable across
ontologies, so the most redundant SAE features
should be the most universal. We set out to test
this and instead learned a methods lesson. Two
innocuous-looking choices in the measurement
pipeline - whether the redundancy ridge R2 is
floored, and whether the partial correlation is taken
on raw values or ranks - each independently manufacture a different headline from the same data.
This paper documents both artifacts, freezes a single defensible estimator, and reports what survives.
The contribution is a reliability result plus a modest,
honestly scoped dissociation; it is not a universality
law.

Background: Natural Latents

Given two observable parts X1 , X2 of a system and
a latent Λ, natural-latent theory (Wentworth and
Lorell, 2025) defines two conditions. Mediation:
X1 ⊥ X2 | Λ, so the parts are independent given
the latent and all shared structure passes through Λ.
Redundancy: Λ is recoverable from each part alone,
H(Λ | Xi ) ≈ 0 for each i. A latent satisfying both
is a natural latent, and the central theorem states
that natural latents are (approximately) isomorphic
across any two agents that agree on the observable
distribution: they are the latents guaranteed to be
translatable, hence universal. The theorem is from
a 2025 preprint; its guarantee is exact for the strict
conditions and approximate with bounded error
otherwise, and we treat it as a motivating anchor
rather than settled fact. Neither condition is a new
primitive: mediation restates conditional independence, with Λ in the graphical role of a common
cause that screens off the parts (Pearl, 2009), and
redundancy is an approximate-determinism or recoverability condition in the information-theoretic
sense of Cover and Thomas (2006), distinct from

Natural latents were introduced by Wentworth and
Lorell (2025); universality and cross-model feature matching are studied by Olah et al. (2020) and
Lan et al. (2024a); SAEs by Bricken et al. (2023);
crosscoders and model diffing by Lindsey et al.
(2024). Most closely related, Lan et al. (2024b)
measure feature-space universality across LLMs
via SAEs, Thasarathan et al. (2025) jointly train a
single cross-model (“universal”) SAE that aligns
concepts across models, and Paulo and Belrose
(2025) show that SAEs trained on the same data
learn different features. We differ in asking a reliability question: whether a single-model, trainingfree redundancy statistic proposed as a predictor of
cross-model transfer survives scrutiny of the estimator itself, and which operationalization of universality it actually tracks. The question of whether
independently trained models converge on shared
representations is broader than SAE feature matching: the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh
et al., 2024) argues that scaling drives models toward a shared statistical representation of reality,
and representational-similarity tools such as CKA
and model stitching (Kornblith et al., 2019) measure such convergence at the level of whole representations rather than individual features. The mediation and redundancy conditions correspond to
conditional independence (Pearl, 2009) and approximate recoverability (Cover and Thomas, 2006) respectively; our notion of redundancy is the naturallatent data-part-recoverability sense, distinct from
decoder-cosine redundancy among SAE latents and
from the redundant-information term of Partial
Information Decomposition (Williams and Beer,
2010).

Two Artifacts

Artifact 1 - the ridge blow-up. For a feature with a large mean and small residual variance, R2 = 1 − ss_res/ss_tot has a tiny denominator, so an imperfect fit yields a huge negative value; on Gemma/Llama-scale activations
the mean redundancy R2 reached ∼ −1.4 × 105 .

pair. Flooring R2 at −1 removes it; a regression
test (test_harness_bug.py) fails pre-fix and
passes post-fix.

What Survives

The dissociation. On the same features and
score, redundancy’s relationship to universality has
opposite sign under the two universality operationalizations: mildly negative for decoder geometry on all three pairs, positive where present for
co-firing (two pairs outside the null, one clearing
the frozen threshold) (Figure 3). Which “universality” you measure decides the answer.

Discussion

The natural-latent prediction, read as a claim about
representational geometry, is falsified in a mild
direction here: more-redundant features are, if anything, slightly less likely to have an aligned decoder
in a second model. The co-firing results suggest
the redundancy signal is not vacuous - it tracks
behavioral overlap on two of three pairs, though
the pre-registered confirmation criterion is met on
only one - but behavioral overlap and geometric
alignment come apart. For practitioners: do not use
within-model redundancy as a cross-model transfer screen, particularly for representation geometry. For methodology: an unfloored ridge and a
raw-control partial are each sufficient to invert a
headline; correlation-over-features interpretability
studies should pre-register the estimator and release
the per-feature arrays.

Limitations
Three pairs only, one layer each (a multi-layer robustness sweep and a third non-Llama-anchored
far pair are the two open blocking runs), so generality beyond these pairs is unestablished. Each
model is represented by a single publicly released
SAE; SAE training is itself unstable across seeds
(Paulo and Belrose, 2025), so SAE-level variance
is unmeasured here. The co-firing universality
measure presupposes that the cross-model feature
matching (token-level max-activation correlation)
is meaningful; if the matching is poor or picks
up superficial co-activation, the co-firing target inherits that weakness. The co-firing measure may
also retain co-activation base-rate structure beyond
the frequency control. We do not resolve why the
Gemma co-firing cell is null while Mistral is positive. The reported bootstrap CIs deviate from the
pre-registered group-level resampling scheme (Section 4) and may be anti-conservative. The redundancy predictor is an in-sample ridge R2 on a PCA
summary, an operational proxy for the theoretical
recoverability condition H(Λ | Xi ) ≈ 0 rather
4

than the full quantity, so a null or negative here
bounds the proxy, not the theorem, whose guarantee requires mediation and redundancy jointly. All
findings are correlational; no causal claim is made.
The two artifacts we document are specific to ridgeR2 redundancy scores and OLS-residualized partial
correlations; other estimator families may harbor
analogous failure modes we do not survey.

Ethics Statement

273

This work uses only publicly released pretrained
language models (GPT-2, Pythia, Gemma-2-9b,
Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) and their publicly released sparse autoencoders, together with public
corpora. It involves no human subjects, no private
or personally identifying data, and no release of any
new model weights. The results require no training
and regenerate from released per-feature arrays on
a CPU. We note a safety-relevant implication: predictors of which interpretability findings transfer
across models bear on the argument that auditing
a small model informs claims about a larger one;
our results caution that such predictors can be artifacts of the estimator, so transfer claims should be
